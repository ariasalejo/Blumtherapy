# Responsible Incentives

## Goal
Help people take practical, self-chosen steps toward health, connection, learning, and meaningful work. Incentives must support autonomy, never buy compliance or punish relapse.

## Potential categories (not currently offered)
- Transport or connectivity for care, education, interviews, or community activities, if funded by a real sponsor.
- Training seats, books, tools, and educational materials.
- Sports, arts, volunteering, peer communities, and recreation.
- Resume/CV support, interview practice, mentoring, and job-readiness workshops.
- Private, non-monetary milestones chosen by the user.
- Basic-needs vouchers through a documented program with transparent eligibility and oversight.
- Formal clinical contingency-management programs only with qualified clinical supervision, protocol, consent, and applicable ethical/legal review.

## Evidence boundary
WHO's 2023 guidance recommends offering cognitive behavioural therapy and contingency management for cocaine and stimulant dependence, while rating the certainty of evidence as low. A [2025 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39545650/) reported moderate-to-large effects for contingency management targeting single-substance use disorders. This supports considering structured, professionally delivered programs—not improvising rewards without clinical design.

## Required safeguards
1. Never condition emergency care, treatment access, or basic rights on milestones.
2. Never remove essential support as punishment for return to use.
3. Never require public testimonials, photos, identity, location, or sensitive medical data for rewards.
4. No gambling, debt, user-funded rewards, coercive contracts, or rewards for falsified tests.
5. Do not ask app users to submit toxicology results. Clinical measurements in formal programs require qualified oversight, informed consent, confidentiality, and protocol.
6. No public leaderboards, shame-based streaks, or moral scoring.
7. Publish sponsor, funding, value, dates, eligibility, limits, selection process, complaint route, and delivery owner.
8. Do not advertise a reward until funding and fulfilment are confirmed.
9. Review equity, fraud, coercion, privacy, and unintended effects with lived-experience contributors and qualified professionals.

Measure service access, education completion, user-chosen goals, continuity of care, satisfaction, employment stability, and safety. Do not treat abstinence as the only meaningful outcome or app clicks as clinical evidence.
