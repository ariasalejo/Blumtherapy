# International Resource Framework

**Research baseline:** 2026-10-12. This is a starting framework, not a complete global directory. Eligibility, crisis contacts, insurance, costs, and availability must be checked locally before publication.

## Official starting points
- **United States:** [FindTreatment.gov](https://www.findtreatment.gov/) — confidential, anonymous locator for mental-health and substance-use treatment. Confirm facility services, availability, eligibility, costs/insurance and contacts. It lists 988 for crisis support and SAMHSA's National Helpline.
- **United Kingdom:** [NHS drug addiction: getting help](https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/addiction-support/drug-addiction-getting-help/) — treatment and support information. Confirm the relevant UK nation and local service.
- **Canada:** [Health Canada: Substance-use treatment](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/substance-use/treatment.html) — treatment options, medication, counselling, peer support and harm reduction. Provincial and territorial access differs.
- **Australia:** [Australian Government Drug Help](https://www.health.gov.au/our-work/drug-help) — official information and support finder; the government lists the National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline at 1800 250 015, 24/7. Reconfirm details before display.
- **Global:** [WHO/UNODC treatment standards](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/international-standards-for-the-treatment-of-drug-use-disorders) — ethical and evidence-informed treatment-system guidance, not a local service directory or endorsement.

## Country-aware data model
Each listing should include country and subregion, language, coverage, organization name/type, official source URL, service type, eligibility, cost/insurance, age restrictions, accessibility, referral requirements, last-checked date, reviewer role, verification status, next review date, and a safe fallback if the resource is unavailable. Do not infer eligibility or availability.

## Localization and crisis safety
Never display one emergency number as universal. If location is unknown, do not guess: point users to their local emergency service or an official country-level directory. Crisis resources must be accessible without an account, manually checked frequently, and clearly labeled with a verification date. Do not imply the app monitors crises or can dispatch help.

## Partnership and incentive discovery
Potential partner categories include public health systems, accredited treatment providers, workforce agencies, vocational education programs, community colleges, employers, recovery housing organizations, libraries, sports and arts programs, food/housing assistance charities, and peer-support organizations. A listing is not a partnership.

Before displaying a material incentive, record the named sponsor, written authorization, confirmed budget, number of places, dates, eligibility, accessibility, selection method, privacy terms, complaint route, and fulfilment owner. Never imply a benefit is available merely because a public program exists. Do not pay users for testimonials or require public disclosure of recovery status.

For any clinical contingency-management program, require qualified clinical leadership, an evidence-informed protocol, consent, privacy protection, and applicable ethics/legal review. A generic app badge or informal coupon is not a substitute for treatment.
